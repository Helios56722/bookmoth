# Bookmoth learner test guide

Use this guide for the first five directional learner sessions. Five sessions can reveal repeated usability problems; they do not prove market demand or broad learning effectiveness.

## Who to invite

Recruit a mix of people who study for school, professional training, or a hobby. Avoid collecting names, student IDs, grades, disability information, or private course records. Participants should use the built-in sample or material they are allowed to share. Do not use an active or proctored assessment.

## Session setup

1. Open Bookmoth and the learner-validation workbench.
2. Assign an anonymous ID such as `L01`.
3. Let the learner work without coaching unless they become blocked.
4. Record observable behavior before asking for opinions.
5. Stop after 15 minutes. Export the session record and store it in the private Hive validation folder.

## Five tasks

1. Load the sample and explain what `S1`, `S2`, and the source pills mean.
2. Find a place where Bookmoth says the source may be unclear or incomplete.
3. Open a practice item, answer it, and trace the answer back to a source.
4. Explain one glow point, thread, or blind spot from the Lantern trail.
5. Export the learning pack as Markdown and describe when that export would be useful.

## Questions after the tasks

- What felt immediately clear?
- Where did you hesitate or need help?
- What would make you trust or distrust a generated explanation?
- Which output would you actually reuse: guide, trail, report, practice, collage, or source review?
- What is missing for your real study workflow?
- Would you use Bookmoth again with your own permitted material? Why?

## First-round decision gate

Continue improving the current product direction when at least four of five learners complete the core flow, at least four understand the source IDs, and at least three say they would reuse Bookmoth for a real permitted learning task. Any privacy concern, misleading confidence, or repeated inability to trace a claim is a priority defect regardless of the totals.

Record the result as directional evidence. Do not describe five sessions as validated demand, proven learning improvement, or willingness to pay.
